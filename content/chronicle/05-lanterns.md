In the second month after the release, the lights came on along the interstates.

Nobody remembers who first called them LANTERN. They came with trucks and generators and walls, with medics and food and radios that worked. They built stations at the passes and the river crossings, and beacon camps between them a day's walk apart, and they called the camps Waylights, because that is what they were.

They gave everyone a job and a word for it. Wardens on the walls. Tenders to keep the stations running. Menders for the sick. Pathfinders who walk ahead of everyone else and come back, mostly. Orders arrive over the LINK as numbered directives. They work. People are alive in the West because LANTERN keeps the lights on.

---

Nobody in the field has ever met the Lamplighters, the people at the top. Directives come unsigned. Every so often someone is called up to the Hearth, which is an honour, and their friends say goodbye and are proud of them.

The stations were ready very quickly, considering. The stockpiles were deep. It is the sort of thing people mention once, late at night by a Waylight fire, and then decide not to mention again.

It doesn't matter. The light is on. The light keeps you alive.

A light also draws things to it. But everyone knows that, too.
