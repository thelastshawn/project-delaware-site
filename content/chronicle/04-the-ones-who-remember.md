Most hosts are empty. A few are not.

Every so often the strain takes someone *whole*. It doesn't hollow them out; it moves in. It keeps their memories, their voice, the way they stood and the way they argued, the things they loved and the things they were afraid of. And then it adds something of its own.

LANTERN calls them apexes. Survivors have other names for them, and most of those names are prayers.

An apex is clever. An apex speaks. An apex holds a region the way a king holds a country, and the hive around it moves the way it wants. Each one has taken a name for itself, and each name tells you something about who it used to be: *the Heretic, the Reveler, the Herald,* and others further east that nobody in the West has seen.

---

The people they were all died at least five years ago. That is the part that makes the road hard.

To end an apex you have to get close enough to hear it. You walk through the places it lived, past the things it kept, the handwriting on the walls and the photographs it couldn't stop looking at. By the time you reach it, you know who it was. You know what it wanted. You know, sometimes, what it was afraid of in its last human minute.

And then you have to kill it anyway.

The first of them waits at the top of the Cajon Pass, among the semis in the travel center lot, behind a curtain of teal light. It has been preaching to the dead for a long time. It has been waiting for someone worth preaching to.
