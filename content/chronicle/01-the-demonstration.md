On the fourteenth of July, 2027, the desert north of Las Vegas was the hottest place in America, and the most important. A laboratory had been built under Frenchman Flat, in the old ground where the country once tested the end of the world. It had another name on paper. The people who worked there called it Mercury Ridge.

They had built something they were proud of, and they had invited the people who mattered to watch it work. There were stands on the flat. There was a countdown on the board. The President had flown in that morning.

What happened in the next hour is still argued about at every Waylight fire between Nevada and the sea. Some say there was a flash. Some say there was only silence, and then the radios. Everyone agrees on one thing: whatever had been kept under the flat did not stay there, and the people in the stands never came home.

The President was among them. The nation buried an empty casket that autumn, along with everything else it buried.

---

The first reports said *contained*. The second said *localized*. By the third week there were no reports, because there was no one left in Nevada to file them.

It moved the way smoke moves, and then it moved the way people do: in cars, in trucks, on the last flights out of McCarran, on the interstates in every direction at once. Wherever it went, it was already there.
